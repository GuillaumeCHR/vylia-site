Reducing your exposure

A poor grade is not a fate. Your exposure surface, everything your apps can know about you, depends as much on your settings as on the apps themselves. Between an app that was granted everything and talks freely to its trackers, and the same app with limited access and filtered trackers, the gap is real.

1

Limit permissions

Refuse location, contacts, microphone or call log when the app does not need them. That is the gap between the grade in this ranking and the one on your phone: on a test phone, Telegram goes from C (50) to B (83) and ChatGPT from D (39) to B (71).

2

Filter trackers

Trackers are in the code: refusing access does not remove them. VYLIA's shield, a local anti-tracker VPN, stops them from reaching their servers, in every app, including the ones you keep. The score measures what the app embeds and does not change.

3

Remove unused apps

A forgotten app keeps its access and its trackers. VYLIA spots the ones you no longer open: uninstalling them reduces your exposure at no cost.

4

Switch to an alternative

For many uses, a more respectful app exists. The alternatives suggested by VYLIA get a median score of 95 in this ranking.

5

Use the web version

Many services work in the browser, installed like an app (WebAPK). The app and its trackers leave the phone, and the browser limits what the site can learn.

6

Pick the lighter version

The same app sometimes exists as a "Lite" or "Go" version, with its own code and therefore its own grade. Facebook Lite gets C (50) against E (0) for Facebook. For the same use, the exposure gain is immediate.

Method

  • Which apps. Sources: Wikipedia, List of most-downloaded Google Play applications (free apps from 500 million to 10 billion downloads); the alternatives of the VYLIA app available on Google Play. Apps from Google, Apple and phone makers (Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, Motorola, vivo, Oppo, Transsion) are ranked apart; carrier apps, system services without a Google Play listing and discontinued apps are left out. Apps from independent publishers pre-installed only on some phones (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Netflix, Office…) are ranked apart, as their download count is inflated.
  • Grade and score. VYLIA grid 3.0 · 505ed919, as in the app: permissions are judged by what the app is for (location costs nothing to a maps app, a lot to a game), trackers by their type.
  • Maximum exposure. Off the phone, nobody knows what you granted: every declared permission is counted as granted. On your phone, an app can get a better grade if you refuse access.
  • Trackers. Signatures found in the code by Exodus Privacy's static analysis, on the latest version published on Google Play. Their presence does not prove their activity, and an app can contain trackers Exodus does not know yet.
  • Limits. The target SDK is unknown (legacy storage access is counted); what the publisher does with data on its servers and security patterns are not measured. Exodus reports from 2021-10-31 to 2026-11-07; "old" flags a report more than a year old.
  • Data. The full details are published under the ODbL 1.0 licence in the VYLIA repository.
